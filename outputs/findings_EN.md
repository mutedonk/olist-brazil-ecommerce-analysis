# Olist E‑Commerce Analysis — Consolidated Findings
> Analysis based only on delivered orders.

## P2 Cohort Analysis - Key Findings
### Core Metrics
- Average lifespan of all customers: 2.6 days
- Average lifespan of active customer: 123.4 days
- Percentage of active customers: 2.13%
- Retention rate within the same month: 1.25%
- Average first‑month retention rate: 0.45%
- Average 3rd‑month retention rate: 0.21%
- Highest retention rate among all cohorts: 0.72%

### Key Insights
1. The 2017‑10 cohort shows abnormally high retention (0.72%), possibly related to pre‑Black‑Friday promotion.
2. Retention drops sharply from the second month by more than 65%, indicating no platform‑driven stickiness.
3. Retention shows no improvement trend across the 18‑month observation window; user loyalty has not been built.

### Business Recommendations
1. Best re‑marketing window is within 30 days after the first order. The same‑month retention rate of 1.25% is already the highest natural return rate.
2. Allocate more budget to customer acquisition during large‑scale promotions such as pre‑Black‑Friday periods, as acquired users demonstrate better quality.
3. Customers with no repurchase within 60 days can be regarded as churned; stop marketing investment for this group.

## P3 RFM Segmentation - Key Findings
### Customer Segmentation
- Lost: 22.58% (21,084 users), contributing only 8.82% of total revenue, reduce marketing investment
- New Customer: 17.67%, recently acquired, focus on triggering repurchase
- One‑time High Value: 12.98%, high spending on a single order, priority for repurchase stimulation
- Dormant High Value: 12.24%, previously high‑spending but now inactive; high priority for win‑back

### Pareto Effect
- Two high‑value groups (Dormant High Value + One‑time High Value) account for 25.2% of customers and generate 47.1% of total revenue.
- VIP Loyal customers make up only 1.25% of the user base with average spend of 407 BRL; they represent the core asset of the platform.

### Actionable Suggestions
1. Send exclusive high‑value coupons to Dormant High‑Value customers; this group brings the highest win‑back ROI.
2. Deliver repurchase incentives to One‑time High‑Value customers within 7 days after their first order to prevent churn.
3. Reduce marketing resources for Lost customers and low‑value one‑time buyers.
4. Build exclusive service mechanisms for VIP Loyal customers.

## P4 Product & Regional Insight - Key Findings
### Core Metrics
1. Category quadrant segmentation:
- **Star categories**: health_beauty, watches_gifts, sports_leisure, computers_accessories. High revenue + high average order value(AOV).
- **Volume category**: bed_bath_table, large order volume with relatively low AOV.
- **Niche categories**: flowers, home_comfort_2, cds_dvds_musicals. Low revenue and small order count.
- **Profit category**: security_and_services, very low order volume but high AOV.

2. State segmentation by AOV
Top 5 high‑AOV states: PB, PA, MT, CE, MS
Low‑AOV states include SP, MG, ES, PR, RS. SP (São Paulo) has the largest absolute order volume and revenue, but its AOV is low.

### Key Insights
1. **Star categories are the core revenue driver**: Health & Beauty and Watches & Gifts are the top two revenue categories. They perform well in both high‑AOV and low‑AOV states, and have higher revenue proportion in high‑AOV states.
2. **Significant category preference gap between high‑AOV and low‑AOV regions**:
    - High‑AOV states spend much more share on health_beauty (+4.23% difference), watches_gifts (+2.32% difference), computers and auto products.
    - Low‑AOV states rely heavily on high‑volume low‑margin categories such as bed_bath_table.
3. **Large market size ≠ high AOV**: São Paulo (SP) is the largest consumption market with massive order quantity, but its average order value is relatively low. High‑AOV states are smaller markets with stronger willingness to purchase premium goods.
4. Niche & profit categories have limited market size. Although some niche products have high unit price, their order count is too small to contribute meaningful revenue.

### Business Recommendations
1. For high‑AOV states (PB, PA, MT, CE, MS): prioritize stocking star categories including health_beauty, watches_gifts and computer accessories. Launch premium product marketing campaigns in these regions to further lift sales.
2. For low‑AOV large‑volume states such as SP and MG: keep sufficient inventory for bed_bath_table and other volume categories. Promote cross‑sell with star category items to raise average order value.
3. Niche categories like flowers and musical CDs can be maintained as supplementary offerings, but do not allocate heavy marketing budget.
4. Security_and_services has high AOV but tiny demand. Keep it as a long‑tail product without expansion.
